# Code of Conduct

GEML is a specification project, so most of the work here is argument: about
syntax, about edge cases, about whether a rule earns its complexity. Sharp
technical disagreement is the point of the place. Hostility toward people is not.

## The standard

**Expected:**

- Argue with the design, the spec text, or the code, and bring a case with you — an
  example, a failing document, a conformance test.
- Assume the other person read the argument in good faith and reached a different
  conclusion.
- Accept that a decision can go against you and still be legitimate.
  [`GOVERNANCE.md`](GOVERNANCE.md) says how decisions are made and how to reopen
  one.

**Not acceptable:**

- Personal attacks, demeaning comments, or sustained hostility toward a
  contributor.
- Harassment of any kind, public or private, including unwelcome attention after
  being asked to stop.
- Discriminatory conduct or language, including about nationality or language
  fluency. This project is bilingual by design; someone writing in their second
  language is not making weaker arguments.
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission.
- Deliberately derailing work: re-litigating a settled decision without new
  evidence, or flooding threads to exhaust reviewers.

## Scope

This applies in every project space — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
messages, code and test comments — and to project-related conduct elsewhere when
it affects people here.

## Reporting

Report privately through the repository's **Security → Report a vulnerability**
form, which is a private channel that reaches the lead maintainer directly; put
"conduct report" at the top so it is not triaged as a vulnerability. A public
issue is the wrong venue for a report about a person.

What happens next:

- **Acknowledgement within 72 hours.**
- The maintainer looks at what was said and done, asks for context where it is
  missing, and responds in one of four ways: a private note asking for a change in
  behaviour; a public correction in the thread; a temporary block from project
  spaces; or a permanent ban.
- Reporters are told the outcome. Details that would identify a reporter are not
  published.

**A limitation, stated rather than hidden:** while the project has a single
maintainer ([`GOVERNANCE.md`](GOVERNANCE.md) treats that as transitional), there
is no independent body to escalate to. A report *about* the lead maintainer can be
raised publicly in an issue, or taken to GitHub Support. Broadening stewardship to
three or more maintainers from more than one party — the target in `GOVERNANCE.md`
— is what fixes this properly.
